# The Ocean's Clock — findings and interpretation

This document is generated by R/07_results.R from the exported analytical tables.

## The data we can actually speak about

The relational joins retain all 6,178 source observations. The natural-reef subset contains 3,569 rows, representing 1,026 subsite-dates across 68 named sites and 102 subsites. These are recorded occurrences with unknown survey effort, not a random sample of reefs or nights.

## 1. Place and season

The atlas reveals uneven coverage. The seasonal calendar shows recorded events concentrated in different months at different sites. Both biological differences and observation schedules can contribute. A zero cell means no recorded event, not a proven non-spawning month.

## 2. The Moon and a fairer count

- Every observation row: **80.3%** of recorded events fall on days 0–7 after the nearest local full moon.
- Every reef-night once: **67.7%** of recorded events fall on days 0–7 after the nearest local full moon.
- Equal weight per subsite: **76.2%** of recorded events fall on days 0–7 after the nearest local full moon.

The lunar concentration remains under all three weighting choices. Its magnitude depends materially on how repeatedly documented nights and sites count. This descriptive window was not a preregistered hypothesis and is not a forecast or causal estimate.

## 3. A nightly schedule, with exceptions

Most displayed genera have their median exact starting time after sunset; Pocillopora in this sample is a morning-spawning exception. The pooled genus comparisons differ in sites, species and coverage, so they cannot isolate a genus effect. Start-quality filtering and collapsing repeated species rows are essential.

At a single Orpheus Island subsite on 24 November 1983, 17 species have exact recorded starts ranging from 0.25 to 3.50 hours after sunset. Shared night does not mean identical minute. This case was deliberately selected for its informative coverage, not as a representative random night.

## 4. Connecting the ocean context

The 2016 temperature case study contains 9 exact date-matched spawning reef-nights and 63 NOAA point extractions across 3 selected subsites and 21 sampled dates. Within each of three named sites, one subsite is selected by its number of recorded 2016 reef-nights (tie: smallest ID). The reference curve connects first-of-month samples; it is not a full daily time series or climate normal.

The combined calendar places season, lunar timing and temperature in one view. Different local thermal cycles provide context; the graphics do not identify a universal temperature threshold or show that temperature caused a timing change. Biological composition and observation schedules differ between sites. All temperature case-study observations are from 2016. NOAA surface-grid values are not coral-depth measurements. A daily UTC grid-date is matched to a recorded local date; this is a daily-date approximation, not an exact local-time ocean measurement.

## Independent reference check

Independent USNO cross-check: 7 recorded reef-nights covered; 7 agree exactly with supplied offsets. This is limited to the cached response coverage, not validation of all years.

Source full-moon offsets remain the primary lunar measure; independent checks do not create a new predictor. The midnight and local-date examples are documented in docs/LEARNING_GUIDE.md and the source/audit tables.

## What makes the work substantive

Separate relational tables; many-to-one key checks; explicit field/ex-situ and start-quality separation; three counting units; independent astronomy coverage reported honestly; coordinate-and-date temperature joins; sensitivity to uneven sampling; genuine dawn events preserved; cached public inputs; reproducible PNGs and their plot-data tables.
